📷 Eclipse Photography Protocol

1. Solar filter ON during all partial phases (before 5:44 PM)
2. Remove filter just before totality begins — shoot corona, Baily's beads, chromosphere
3. Actually look with your own eyes for at least 30 seconds — once in a lifetime
4. Replace filter IMMEDIATELY when totality ends
5. Set intervalometer to auto-shoot during totality (don't manually fiddle)
6. Write settings on an index card and tape to tripod leg

📋 Suggested Totality Settings (tape to tripod)

Partial phases (filter on): ISO 100 · f/8 · 1/1000s
Totality — corona wide: ISO 400 · f/5.6 · 1/250s → bracket ±2 stops
Totality — corona close (telephoto): ISO 400 · f/8 · 1/500s → bracket
Baily's beads / diamond ring: ISO 100 · f/8 · 1/2000–1/4000s · filter OFF
Set intervalometer for 2s intervals, let it run — focus on watching

Weather Contingency

Iceland's northwest coast can be cloudy. If Hellissandur forecast looks poor on Aug 10–11, consider day trip to Ólafsvík (~20 min east, same totality zone) or push further east toward Borgarnes. Do NOT chase totality into the Westfjords — too far, too remote. The ring road south of Snæfellsnes is your backup mobility corridor.

Photography Highlights

August light: Golden-hour quality light ~10 PM–2 AM. Having the campervan means you can be at the right waterfall at midnight. Build sleep schedule around this.
Tier 1 — Non-Negotiables

🧊 Jökulsárlón Glacier Lagoon + Diamond Beach

Icebergs in the lagoon, ice chunks on black volcanic sand at Diamond Beach. Best at blue hour (10 PM–midnight or 4–6 AM). Plan an early alarm for Aug 14.

💧 Seljalandsfoss + Skógafoss

Seljalandsfoss: walk behind the waterfall — gets you wet but worth it. Skógafoss: massive 60m drop, rainbow in afternoon light. Both on Aug 13 en route south.

🏔️ Kirkjufell + Kirkjufellsfoss

Most photographed mountain in Iceland. Also ideal eclipse foreground (Aug 7 scouting). Kirkjufellsfoss in foreground with mountain behind — long exposure at blue hour.

🗿 Svartifoss

Waterfall framed by hexagonal basalt columns in Skaftafell / Vatnajökull NP. 30-min hike in. Aug 14 area stop.

Tier 2 — Worth It If Time Allows

⛰️ Mt. Vestrahorn / Stokksnes

Dramatic peak reflected in black sand dune pools. Near Höfn on the southeast coast. Small fee for access. Potential Aug 14 stop.

🦜 Dyrhólaey

Clifftop lighthouse with ocean arch below. Puffins most active evenings. Also viewpoint for Reynisfjara (check storm access status).

🌿 Eldhraun Lava Field

Stunning moss-covered lava field on south coast. Photograph from the road — do NOT walk on the moss (permanent damage, takes centuries to regrow).

🌊 Goðafoss — Waterfall of the Gods

Near Lake Mývatn. If detouring northeast for eclipse weather contingency, this is a worthy stop. Wide semicircular drop.

⚠️ 2026 Note: Reynisfjara beach basalt columns were damaged in a 2026 storm. Check current access status before planning this stop.

Birdwatching

🐦 Puffins

Present through end of August — you're right in the window. Most active evenings when they return from fishing. Best locations: Dyrhólaey (dramatic cliffs, may have restricted access — check before visiting), Ingólfshöfði (guided tractor tour across lagoon — closes mid-August, confirm dates), Arnarstapi on Snæfellsnes (accessible, ~60 km west of the base). Also seen on Reykjanes Peninsula cliffs.

🐦 Arctic Terns

Everywhere in Iceland in August. Will aggressively dive-bomb your head if you walk near nests — bring a stick to hold overhead (locals do this). More funny than dangerous. Best for photography when they're hovering into the wind.

🦆 Lake Mývatn

13 duck species breed here — one of the most diverse duck habitats in the world. If you make the northeast detour (eclipse weather contingency), Mývatn is worth a few hours. Also: possible gyrfalcon sightings. Midges are intense in June–July but thin by August.

🦅 Other Species

Guillemots, fulmars, razorbills — cliff nesters, easily seen at Dyrhólaey, Látrabjarg (Westfjords — not in our route), Arnarstapi.
Rock ptarmigan — upland lava fields and Snæfellsjökull area.
Merlin — small falcon, seen on open moorland.
Gyrfalcon — Reykjanes Peninsula and Mývatn. Iceland's largest falcon.
Golden plover — considered the herald of spring in Iceland, still present in August on moorlands.
